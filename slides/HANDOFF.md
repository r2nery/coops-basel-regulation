# Handoff: ICA CCR Europe 2026 deck (Basel paper)

Written 2026-11-03 at the end of a long working session. Read this first, then `slides/deck.tex`.

## What this is

Ten-minute talk (notes currently total about 13 minutes) for the ICA CCR Europe Research
Conference, Valencia, 4 November 2026,  Paper: "Institutional profiles
under a common prudential framework" (Nery, Victorino, Rangel), repo
`~/Documents/GitHub/coops-basel-regulation` (public on GitHub, r2nery/coops-basel-regulation).
Brazilian credit cooperatives (130) against banks (144) under the full Basel methodology,
IF.data, 2017 to 2024, 7,234 institution-quarters; 339 other non-cooperatives excluded from the
main comparison.

## Current deck (16 slides, no supplement)

1. Title (ICA CCR Europe Research Conference; logos: Sistema OCB + ICA CCR, `figures/logo_ica_ccr.png`)
2. Prudential framework (tikz; caption: common prudential framework is the selection criterion)
3. Sample and comparison group: scatter of institution medians, credit vs funds raised
   (`make_broad_group_slide.py` -> `s_broad_group.png`); numbers 130 / 144 / 339; card "Why compare to banks"
4. Four specifications (`make_spec_variants.py` -> `s_specs.png`, version "scatter"): size
   densities on top, scatter + fitted lines below, arrows = after-tax ROA gap +1.8/+2.1/+1.8/+1.6;
   specs: (1) naive, (2) size and quarter controls, (3) matched on size (CEM size quintile), (4) common support [11.39, 15.68]
5-9. One slide per outcome (`make_outcome_slides.py` -> `s_out_<key>.png` + `tables/spec_<key>.tex`):
   scatter vs size with marginal densities, table of the four specs (averages), short reading.
   Titles state the finding: credit (+16.5..+18.8 pp), volatility (-1.1..-1.9, institution level, HC3),
   z-score (+4.2..+6.3, with formula, Hesse and Cihak 2007), ROA after tax (+1.6..+2.1),
   ROA before tax (significant only in (1) and (2)).
10. On the taxation of cooperatives (`make_tax_slide.py` -> `s_tax_simple.png`): four bars,
   median ROA under (2) for cooperatives vs banks of the same size: before 3.2 vs 1.8, after 2.8 vs 1.1;
   three bullets (cooperative act rationale; surplus partly members paying above cost;
   retained surplus goes to indivisible reserves, main source of capital). Tax rates: banks 35%, coops 1%.
11. Result 1. Eleven outcomes at comparable size (`make_profile_slide.py` -> `profile.png`),
   cards Stable / Stable in sign / Null. Note frames it as "here they are together as a single result".
12. Result 2. Capital: the average overstates the typical gap (`make_capital_slide.py` ->
   `s_capital_dist.png`): KDE on log scale, braces "medians coops +2.1", "means banks +8.2".
   Agreed phrase everywhere: the typical cooperative holds about as much capital as the typical bank.
   At equal size: -15.8 mean, -2.2 median (in the speaker note only).
13. Result 3. Provisioning and margin depend on the comparison group (`make_group_slide.py` ->
   `s_group_ladder.png`): all non-coops / banks / commercial / deposit-funded.
14. Open question: region and lending (`make_region_slide.py` -> `s_region.png`): both bars CEM (3),
   +17 nationally, -3 within region (n.s., ~18 effective control banks).
15. Brazil matches the literature except on profitability (table: theory / Brazil / cross-country;
   Hesse and Cihak 2007, Beck et al. 2009, Cook 1995, Fonteyne 2007).
16. Summary (Profile, Capital, Comparison group; For regulators; Limits).
